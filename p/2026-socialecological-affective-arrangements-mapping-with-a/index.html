Socialecological affective arrangements: mapping with a small sea of many seas

Violeta Cabello, Xabier Barandiaran, Blanca Gonzalez-Mon, Juan Manuel Zaragoza, Jesús Mario Siqueiros, Marcela Brugnach (2026)

Abstract

Environmental degradation engenders deep affective experiences that shape how people explain its causes and respond to it, yet socialecological research lacks tools to analyse these affective relations alongside factual accounts. This article proposes the concept of socialecological affective arrangement (SEAA), a historically and geographically situated formation of entities and practices that affect, and are affected by, one another, together with a method to map it: the mapparatus. The mapparatus codes interview narratives into networks whose nodes are narrators, entities (from nitrogen and algae to laws) and practices, and whose links are affects and emotions. It distinguishes egoaffective relations, through which narrators are themselves affected, from alteraffective relations, through which they explain what affects others, and combines network analysis with close qualitative reading. The method is explored through eight interviews with farmers, biologists, a fisher and a researcher concerned with the eutrophication of the Mar Menor, a Spanish coastal lagoon and the first ecosystem in Europe granted legal personhood. Participants are divided yet connected. Farmers cohere around how new regulations and public blame affect them; non-farmers cohere in signalling agriculture as a direct cause of eutrophication. Shared attachments to the lagoon, such as bathing and sailing with family, a common frustration with its governance and a participant experiencing inner affective contradictions about the lagoon connect both groups. Mapping SEAAs foregrounds affective experience as a crucial source of socialecological information and helps explain why people know-feel different worlds in contested environmental problems, offering a tool for affective knowing in sustainability research.
